# Third-party notices

The notices distributed with the Android app are in `src/Centello.App/Resources/Raw/` and are shown on the `Licenze` screen:

- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`: one entry per bundled library and font, with license texts and links to publisher terms and upstream notices. Each license heading is framed by rules of dashes, which is how the app tells it apart from the license text.
- `DOTNET_RUNTIME_THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` and `DOTNET_ANDROID_THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`: verbatim copies of the `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.TXT` files of the `Microsoft.NETCore.App.Runtime.Mono.android-arm64` and `Microsoft.Android.Runtime.Mono.36.android-arm64` packs, covering what the runtimes compile in.

The main Glide 4.16.0 notice uses the full upstream BSD 2-Clause terms, including Google's final paragraph. DiskLruCache and the GIF decoder have separate entries. [Glide 4.16.0 license](https://github.com/bumptech/glide/blob/v4.16.0/LICENSE)

Protobuf Lite 4.28.2 (upstream tag v28.2) is shaded in Tink Android. AndroidX DataStore 1.1.7 also embeds relocated Protobuf classes under BSD-3-Clause. ThreeTenBP contributed code to the Kotlin 2.2.0 standard library. The two BSD-3-Clause entries have separate copyright notices and license texts. [Protobuf v28.2 license](https://github.com/protocolbuffers/protobuf/blob/v28.2/LICENSE) · [Kotlin 2.2.0 ThreeTenBP license](https://github.com/JetBrains/kotlin/blob/v2.2.0/license/third_party/threetenbp_license.txt)

The Kotlin 2.2.0 standard library also includes implementations of `asinh`, `acosh` and `atanh` derived from Boost. Centello includes the attribution and Boost Software License 1.0 for reference. The license's notice and license-text requirement has an exception for copies and derivative works solely in the form of machine-executable object code generated by a source language processor. [Kotlin 2.2.0 attributions](https://github.com/JetBrains/kotlin/blob/v2.2.0/license/README.md) · [Boost license as bundled by Kotlin](https://github.com/JetBrains/kotlin/blob/v2.2.0/license/third_party/boost_LICENSE.txt)

The Android AI integration takes Firebase AI 18.2.0 and App Check 20.1.0 from Firebase Android BoM 35.2.0. The Debug and development variants use the debug App Check provider. The Play variant uses the Play Integrity provider. The binding's [FirebaseDependencies.props](src/Centello.FirebaseAi.Android/FirebaseDependencies.props) lists the pinned native artifacts.

Firebase AI, App Check and most of the Firebase libraries use Apache 2.0. The Maven POMs identify Google Play services and Firebase Auth Interop under the Android Software Development Kit License. Play Integrity and Play Core Common name their own Google Play terms. The app’s notice asset links to those terms and identifies the upstream AAR notices. [Google Maven](https://maven.google.com/) · [Android SDK terms](https://developer.android.com/studio/terms) · [Play Integrity terms](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/terms) · [Play Core terms](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/license)

Review and update the notices whenever dependencies, assets or the .NET SDK change. Review NuGet dependencies in `src/Centello.App/obj/project.assets.json` after a restore. For Android Maven dependencies, check the binding’s pinned artifacts, POM licenses, and notices embedded in the AARs and JARs for the variant you ship.
